   classifies a release as a patch, and the host, which takes such a release without acting.
4. "A **minor** grows what a host may adopt in a backward-compatible way: a new capability, a new law,

   as a minor, and the host, which re-runs its catch-up walk to take it.

   adoption or catch-up step, a moved law that forces host action." (`SKILL.md:560-562`) — reaches the
   releasing session, which classifies a release as a major.
6. "A major ships its dated `MIGRATION.md` chapter [INV-91]." (`SKILL.md:563-564`) — reaches the
   releasing session, which must write the dated `MIGRATION.md` chapter for a major release.
7. "The default is a patch. It is raised to a minor or a major only where the release earns the higher
   tier." (`SKILL.md:564-565`) — reaches the releasing session, which sets the default tier and the bar
   for raising it.
8. "This is a judgment the releasing session makes and states, **held by no machine**." (`SKILL.md:565-566`)
   — reaches the releasing session, which alone makes and states the call, and any gate or script that
   might otherwise be built to make it, which is told not to.
9. "The minor-versus-major call reads meaning a gate cannot, so it **stays a stated rule the session
   holds**, the same standing as a design-review finding that never blocks a lane [INV-141]."
   (`SKILL.md:566-568`) — reaches the releasing session and the push/release gate machinery, which is
   told this call never blocks a lane.
10. "It keeps its published number as this cited boundary case." (`SKILL.md:577-578`, of the 2.0.0
    release) — reaches anyone who later audits or would consider renumbering the 2.0.0 release, telling
    them its published major number stands even though it reads as a minor under this rule.
