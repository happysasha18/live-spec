## The requirements

Ten demands, each quoted from the rule's own words. Every row names who the demand reaches and the
machine that holds it. It also names the evidence of a real catch, and the nearest place the pack
says it again.

### 1. A marked item is re-tested for derivability at every touch

**Quote.** "A backlog item carrying a needs-the-human's-word marker is re-tested by derivability
every time it is touched". The rule adds: "at its first writing and at every touch after."

**Reaches.** Any seat that writes or touches a parked backlog item, in any project on the pack.

**Machine.** None tests derivability. `guardrails/check-deferral-marker.py` tests whether a parked
item names a reason word. The chat-law hook re-fires the reminder at the moment of writing
(`hooks/chat-law-hook.sh:18`), and that hook reminds without blocking.

**Evidence.** The rule's prose form failed three times in one morning on 2026-07-15, each failure
dated and named in `JOURNAL.md:977-983`. Those three are the reason the mechanical arm was built.

**Said elsewhere.** Rule 27 carries the same demand in one sentence: "It never parks derivable work
on the human's queue to avoid deciding [INV-4]" (`skills/live-spec-base/SKILL.md:454`).

### 2. Three surfaces carry such a marker

**Quote.** "Three things carry such a marker: a queue row held for his word, a `NEXT_STEPS.md` line,
and a decision a setup script leaves open."

**Reaches.** A seat writing in the queue, in the resume file, or in a setup script's decision page.

**Machine.** Partial. `guardrails/pre-commit:33-43` runs the gate over `NEXT_STEPS.md` and
`docs/decisions/*.md` alone. A held queue row in `ROADMAP.md` is scanned by no gate. A setup script's
open decision is scanned only where it lands in `docs/decisions/`.

**Evidence.** One of the three dated 2026-07-15 failures was a version bump citing "row 231
reserved", a queue row (`JOURNAL.md:980-981`). The surface that produced that failure is the one
surface the gate still does not read.

**Said elsewhere.** No other rule enumerates these three carriers.

### 3. A pinned answer makes the item the seat's own

**Quote.** "Sometimes the answer pins to an existing artifact: a base rule, a spec sentence, the
architecture, an approved prototype, or an already-answered decision [INV-59]. The item is then the
seat's. Do it, cite the artifact, and drop the marker [INV-121, INV-143]."

**Reaches.** The seat holding the parked item.

**Machine.** None. No gate checks that a dropped marker leaves a citation behind, and no gate checks
whether a standing marker had a pinning artifact available.

**Evidence.** None on record for a machine catch. The requirement's own worth rests on the three
dated 2026-07-15 incidents.

**Said elsewhere.** Rule 1 states the same derivation duty: "before offering the human a fork, check
whether a proven artifact already settles it... Say it back with the section cited as its ground,
offering no fork" (`skills/live-spec-base/SKILL.md:116-118`, INV-121).

### 4. A fact no artifact holds keeps the item with the human

**Quote.** "Where it needs a fact no artifact holds — a taste, a policy, or an act irreversible
outside git (rule 17)". The sentence closes: "it is the human's, and the marker stands."

**Reaches.** The seat deciding whether to park, and the human who receives the parked item.

**Machine.** None judges whether the named fact is genuine. The gate accepts any word from its
reason list.

**Evidence.** None on record.

**Said elsewhere.** Rule 17 owns the irreversible-act clause, and rule 29 points at it by name. Rule
27 lists the same three qualifying cases for surfacing a decision. Its words are "a taste call, a
trade-off no artifact settles [INV-121], or a change to the definition of correct"
(`skills/live-spec-base/SKILL.md:452-453`). Rule 12 states the same routing for the same categories.

### 5. Writing a marker requires naming the human-only fact

**Quote.** "Writing such a marker requires naming that human-only fact. A marker that cannot name it
defaults to the seat's."

**Reaches.** The seat writing the marker, at commit time.

**Machine.** `guardrails/check-deferral-marker.py`, wired at `guardrails/pre-commit:41`. It folds a
bullet with its wrapped lines into one item, and it finds parks by grammatical shape. It reds a
commit where the item names no reason category.

**Evidence.** The gate's own dated defect is on record. On 2026-07-17 an adversarial review found the
`⟨DECIDE⟩` marker walking straight through the gate that polices open decisions
(`guardrails/check-deferral-marker.py:15-19`, `JOURNAL.md:572`). The fix landed with its tests. No
record shows this gate blocking a real commit after it landed.

**Said elsewhere.** The spec restates it at `PRODUCT_SPEC.md:5126`, and `TEST_MATRIX.md:177` restates
the whole rule again inside row M-297.

### 6. An unjustified marker is itself the finding

**Quote.** "A marker that cannot say why the item belongs to the human is itself the finding."

**Reaches.** The seat reading the parked item, and any reviewer sweeping the resume file.

**Machine.** The same gate prints one finding line per offending item, with path and line number
(`guardrails/check-deferral-marker.py:216`). Its reach is the two file kinds named in
requirement 2.

**Evidence.** Same as requirement 5.

**Said elsewhere.** `skills/build-pipeline/SKILL.md:174` states the identical finding shape for a
request matching no kind, and names rule 29's clause as one of its four siblings.

### 7. The posture binds the orchestrator seat at any tier

**Quote.** "This is the no-homeless-item control — rule 27's decide-what-it-can-decide posture

**Reaches.** Every seat in the orchestrator role, on Opus, Sonnet, or Haiku.

**Machine.** None.

**Evidence.** None on record.

**Said elsewhere.** This sentence is a pointer to rule 27, and it says so in its own words. Rule 27
