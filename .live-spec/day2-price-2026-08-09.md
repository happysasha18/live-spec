## The price sits in the reviews, and this is the finding

Two standing gates decide what a cut costs.

Gate a refuses a push whose `PRODUCT_SPEC.md` change carries no adversarial review record newer
than the change. Every cut edits the spec, since a removed thing has a spec row.

Gate s refuses a push whose skill body changed without a skill-creator review record newer than the
change. Every rule cut edits `skills/live-spec-base/SKILL.md`, since that file is where the rules
live.

So a rule cut owes both reviews by construction. Tonight the two reviews cost 12 minutes and 10
minutes of a fresh agent each, and they were worth their price: the adversarial review returned
four blocking findings and six major ones, and the skill review refused two drafts of the repaired
rule before passing the third. One of its findings overturned a verdict of this seat.

## The lever that keeps days 4 to 10 intact

Both gates read the push range. A batch of cuts landing in one push needs one adversarial review and
one skill review between them, so the review cost divides across the batch.

The plan already puts one batch on each of days 4 to 10, and it says one cut is one commit. Those
two hold together: many commits, one push a day, one review pass over that push.

Recounted from tonight's two samples, a batch of three rules costs about two and a quarter hours:
roughly twenty-five minutes for each rule's own tail, half an hour for the shared adversarial
review, and half an hour for the shared skill review. Seven days at one batch a day carries about
twenty rules, against thirty-five in the file today.

census counts 123 test files and 7 368 document lines across all thirty-five rules. Day 4's first
