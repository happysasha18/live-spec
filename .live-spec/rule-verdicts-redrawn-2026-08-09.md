## What reading all thirty-five rules changed

The plan's third stage removes rules, one batch a day, thickest files first, to bring down the 45 000
tokens a session reads before work. Reading every rule against the criterion says the volume sits
somewhere else than the rule count.

**Two rules hold a quarter of the rulebook.** Rule 31, on how agents talk to each other, is 6 067
bytes. Rule 7, on concurrent edits and worker restores, is 5 477 bytes. Together they are 11 544 of
the 48 387 bytes of rule text, near a quarter of it. The eleven largest rules hold 28 730 bytes,
which is 59 per cent.

**Most rules reach a stranger or reach his reports.** Of the thirty-five, twenty-six do one or the
other by the criterion's own words. Deleting rules is therefore a small lever on the measure, and it
spends the criterion's protection to get there.

**So the work that moves the measure is shortening, and the rules to shorten are the fat ones.**
Rule 7 states one law and then twelve worked cases. Rule 31 states one law and then a protocol. Both
can state their law and send the cases to a reference page a session loads on demand, which is the
shape the pack already uses for skill references. A session then reads the law and pays for the cases
only when it needs them.
