# Where the rules came from

The dated words, the incidents, and the reasons behind eight of the twenty-two rules, plus the intro's
glossary pointer and one writing-register rule. The operational text of every rule stays in `SKILL.md`;
this file is read when a rule's ORIGIN is wanted — why it was asked for, what it cost the day it was
missing — and not before. Every passage below reads exactly as it read in the rule's body.

The q-536 rulings (below, dated 2026-09-01) are a second kind of origin: not why a rule was asked for,
but why its wording changed. The 07-30 sweep read the body's rules against each other and found
fourteen small collisions — a figure repeated in two places, a bullet's scope read wider than meant, two
rules both claiming the same territory. Each ruling names the collision found and the rewrite that
closed it.

## Rule 12 — the capture echo

No echo ⇒ the human cannot know the request survived.

Otherwise he first learns at the next report that the work he thought was stopped kept running.

(SPEC INV-93; his 2026-07-10 word at the release: say how long it takes, then track it. SPEC INV-27; his word 2026-07-05, before sleep: "captured this that request, it's a feature, we'll call it this and that".)

q-536 ruling, 2026-09-01: the 07-30 sweep read a critical wish arriving silently (an inbox file, a
harvest) as also owed an immediate interruption, on top of its echo in the next report. Closed by
rewrite — only the bug door preempts, stated two bullets up in the rule's critical-priority clause, and
the silent-arrival bullet overlaps none of it.

q-536 ruling, 2026-09-01: the 07-30 sweep flagged the range's "~0 wall-clock" figure (for read-only
checks and disjoint-file workers running alongside the critical path) as ungrounded, and colliding with
rule 9's lane cap. Closed by rewrite — the figure is grounded in the critical-path reasoning stated
just above it, and it describes read-only/disjoint-file overlap, not the in-work lane count rule 9
caps.

(his third ask in the family, 2026-07-06 evening — the delivery reports were good, the mid-work trail thin)

a reader dropping in mid-session can tell what is being worked without scrolling back

q-536 ruling, 2026-09-01: an exact "10 minute" figure once sat in a glossary beside this rule's own
"~10 minutes" for the Heartbeat tooth. Closed by rewrite — the glossary is gone (moved to

## Rule 10 — the one interactive decision page

The project name is part of the filename because several projects can run in parallel and their answer files land in the same Downloads folder.

(Born 2026-07-05 from tuning images the same way.)

q-536 ruling, 2026-09-01: the 07-30 sweep found the glossary sending a reader to this rule for the
