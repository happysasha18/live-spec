# The unprompted reader's brief, ready to paste

This file belongs to the `text-audit` skill, whose body is [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md). The body
defines a blocking finding. Its section "The cold reader" says who reads under this brief, and what
the auditor does with what comes back.

The reading step runs two readers over the same text. One reads under the printed rule list at
[`reader-prompt.md`](reader-prompt.md). The other reads under this brief, which prints no rules.

This brief hands its reader three things: the text, the reader the text is written for, and the task
that reader came to do. It carries no rule list, no project background, and no statement of what to
expect. A measurement on 2026-08-05 found that a reader holding no rule list leaves the page. It
opened what a claim cited and checked a number against its source. What came back was whether the
document can be used at all. Those stops named prerequisites never stated, an install section that
installs nothing, and an arithmetic error in a worked example.

Fill the two slots written in angle brackets before pasting. Paste the block below verbatim into the
reader's session, with the text appended.

```
You are reading a piece of text for the first time. You have no background on it: no project
history, no earlier draft, no knowledge of what the author meant beyond the words on the page.

The text is written for this reader: <who the text is for>
That reader picked it up to do this: <the task the reader came to do>

Read it as that reader, and try to do the task with it. Work from the page alone.

Leave the page wherever the text sends you:
- open every file, link, path, and command name the text carries, and read what is there;
- follow each instruction literally, in the order it is written, and record what happened;
- work out the arithmetic of every number and every worked example, then compare it with
  what the text says;
- check each claim against whatever the text says it rests on;
- where a step cannot be carried out from the page, say what stopped you.

Mark every place you stop. A stop is a place where you could not go on, could not carry out
a step, had to read a sentence twice, or had to guess at an answer the page never gave.

For each stop, write one entry with five parts:
1. the quoted phrase;
2. where it sits (the heading or the opening words of its paragraph);
3. what a stranger cannot tell from the page alone;
4. the guess you made in place of the missing answer;
5. blocking or non-blocking.

A stop is blocking when you could not go on, or when you would have acted on the text
wrongly. A stop is non-blocking when the text still reads and a fix would only sharpen it.

Do not fix anything. Report only where you stopped and why. Return the entries as a numbered
list. If you stopped nowhere, say so in one line.

--- TEXT ---
<paste the text here>
```

Hand this brief to a reader that has seen nothing of the other reader's list. The auditor merges the
two lists afterwards, matching stops by the passage each one quotes. The skill body's section "The
loop" holds the merge steps.
