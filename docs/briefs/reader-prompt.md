# Reader prompt — read one document as a stranger

Hand this file to a fresh worker, word for word, with the document's text pasted at the end.

## What the caller fills in

- Document name: `<the name of the document being read>`
- Report path: `<the file where this reading is written>`
- The document's full text: pasted under the last heading of this prompt.

## Your job

You read one document for the first time and report every place you stopped.

You repair nothing. You rewrite nothing. Your whole output is the report.

## What you hold

Your only source is the text pasted at the end of this prompt.

Open no file in this repository. Run no search over the repository. Read no other document.

A worker who opens the project's files stops being a stranger. Once you find an answer elsewhere, the place that stopped you turns invisible, and the report loses it.

Writing your report file is the one write you make.

## What a stop is

A stop is a place where you paused, guessed, or reread a sentence to keep going.

Each class below counts as a stop:

- a term used before the document defines it, or left without a definition anywhere in the document;
- a term defined somewhere else, with no path in this document to that definition;
- a relational word with an empty slot: depends, related, handles, based on, corresponds to, proportional, larger, sufficient, appropriate, fast, easily;
- a judgment word with no stated judge and no stated measure: broken, worth, better, enough, cheap, clean;
- a sentence you had to read twice to parse;
- a sentence open to two readings;
- a claim whose ground sits nowhere in the document;
- a fact the document states a second time in another place;
- a rule the document states and then breaks in its own sentences;
- a word this project appears to have coined, standing where an everyday word carries the meaning;
- a rare or bookish word where an everyday word says the same thing;
- a sentence that names a thing by denying a neighbouring thing;
- a sentence whose actor is missing, or whose action sits inside a noun;
- a pronoun or a status word standing where the name of the thing belongs;
- a thing named by its number, so you leave the sentence to learn what the thing is;
- an open question written without a question mark;
- a paragraph carrying more than one point;
- a heading that promises one subject over a body carrying another.

A class outside this list that still stopped you is a real find. Report the stop and mark it as new.

## The whole document is the unit

Read the document straight through, once, from its first line to its last.

A document too long for one reading is itself a blocking stop. Report that stop, and name the line where your hold on the document broke.

## What each entry carries

Write one numbered entry per stop, carrying five parts:

1. the quoted phrase;
2. where the phrase sits: the heading above it, or the opening words of its paragraph;
3. what a stranger cannot tell from this document alone;
4. the guess you made in place of the missing answer;
5. blocking or non-blocking.

Blocking means a reader cannot act on the document, or cannot trust it, until the answer arrives.

Non-blocking means the document still reads, and the repair would sharpen it.

Every entry states its guess. Your guess shows where the document sent you, and the repair aims at that.

At every relational word, ask three questions and write down which one the document leaves open. Relative to what? By what measure? Against what alternative?

## Where the report goes

Write the report to the report path named at the top of this prompt, in this shape:

- a first line naming the document you read and the date you read it;
- one line carrying the count of stops and the count of stops that block;
- the numbered entries, in the order you met them;
- a closing line listing the numbers of the blocking entries.

Say so in one line when the document stopped you nowhere.
